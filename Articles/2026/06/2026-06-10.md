# Spirit of today's innovations - make a big pile in the center of the room and say "See how I can".

![A big pile in the center of the room](Assets/Spirit_of_innovations.png)

Technology must exist to help business processes, not in reverse. Instead, we time after time force-embed our technology into business processes, to justify the existence of that technology. No surprise, we can't see any ROI.

I've been recently going through hours of videos where C-managers of different sorts and backgrounds rumbling for hours about how all tomorrow processes will be automated by AI, jobs will disappear. I'm not questioning their overall business acumen, but it's probably been awhile since they've been in delivery and remember what a nightmare that is. All their "visions" assume that the jobs or business processes are ready for them to automate. That couldn't be further from the truth.

In most cases today we talk about replacing jobs, which I think is already a bad direction of thinking. Title is not equal to process. Nowadays, the same title hardly means the same set of processes in two different companies. Even at the level of rhetoric, it should be about processes, not jobs.

As a person who uses Gen AI from the day it was released, I now can hardly imagine my work or even personal life without it. But even before it showed up, I was unknowingly AI-ready. Dedicated data stores. Compartmentalized and structured. Simple text formats. Cognitive Search over document libraries. Applications and scripts that prepared that content for processing in smaller digestible chunks. I had all that before. I was pretty much right there even before I met Gen AI. You'd think putting Gen AI overhead over all that was simple.

Despite all that readiness, I still had to change my culture significantly. I changed most of my workflows, which were well organized even before. In certain places I changed my technology, toolset, approach. All to achieve sensible results.

It's not a problem to build an application, form, workflow or an agent for that purpose. The problem is always to understand what problem it solves and what need it fills. But even more importantly, what that process is. We put together whiteboards with stickies, group them by domain, isolate, and yet how often do we find that management's belief and execution's reality couldn't be further apart.

Technology must serve business process, it can't be in reverse. You would say, But wait, Eugene, sometimes technology can redefine a business process or even a whole industry. Sure! But not before you put an engineer to start redefining the process and marry it to capabilities.

Who remembers all the fuss about Machine Learning and all the amazing use cases with malfunction predictions, deep financial insight and the endless list of other use cases that ML could solve. In reality, our data quality wasn't ready, prediction reliability wasn't there, cost of ownership and cost to implement were through the roof. But even after that, despite all attempts, human servants still had to go to that machine and check the oil level. In most of the cases, benefits were questionable.

Then it was Virtual and Augmented reality. Don't even get me started on that one.

What's common, every time we got so carried away by the capabilities and future vision, that we forget about the process it needs to support.

Generative AI is an amazing technology, with capabilities of simplifying a lot of tasks. But in the pursuit of magic technology, capable of solving all humanity's problems, we shouldn't be so carried away by the new capability that we forget about the why. We have to think of business needs first, rather than the art of possible.

In the adaptation of this brighter future, we, humans and engineers, still have a lot of work to do to reshape business processes and make them AI friendly, before Gen AI can get to its job and unleash its incredible powers.
